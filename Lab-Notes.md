Historical Development of AI 

Humans have dreamed of creating thinking machines from ancient times. Folklore and historical attempts to build programmable devices reflect this long-standing ambition and fiction abounds with the possibilities of intelligent machines, imagining their benefits and dangers. It’s no wonder that when OpenAI introduced GPT-1 (Generative Pre-trained Transformer) in 2018, it marked an important technical step in the development of generative AI and the realization of this ancient dream.

The 2020 launch of GPT-3 was a landmark moment in artificial intelligence due to its unprecedented size, featuring 175 billion parameters, which enabled it to perform a wide range of natural language tasks without extensive fine-tuning. This model was trained using large amounts of text data, allowing it to generate human-like text and perform few-shot learning. Its capabilities helped demonstrate the potential of large language models (LLMs) for applications such as chatbots and virtual assistants.

Later advances brought these technologies to a much wider audience. In 2022, ChatGPT, based on GPT-3.5, brought generative AI into the mainstream. GPT-4, released in 2023, further expanded public interest in what AI could do.

Today, AI is embedded into many aspects of daily life, from social media to work processes. As the technology improves, its influence is likely to grow. To understand the directions the technology can take, it helps to understand how we got here. Here is a history of major developments in AI.

In 1726, Jonathan Swift’s fantastic novel Gulliver’s Travels introduces the idea of The Engine, a large mechanical contraption used to assist scholars in generating new ideas, sentences and books.

Scholars turn handles on the machine, which rotates wooden blocks inscribed with words. The machine is said to create new ideas and philosophical treatises by combining words in different arrangements:

“Every one knew how laborious the usual method is of attaining to arts and sciences; whereas by his contrivance the most ignorant person, at a reasonable charge and with a little bodily labour, might write books in philosophy, poetry, politics, laws, mathematics and theology, without the least assistance from genius or study.”

- Jonathan Swift’s Gulliver’s Travels (1726)

Swift’s satire anticipates the concept of algorithmic text generation, which is now a reality with modern AI. AI models can produce coherent text by combining words and ideas based on underlying algorithms, similar to what Swift’s fictional Engine is meant to do.

In 1914,Spanish engineer Leonardo Torres y Quevedo demonstrates the first chess-playing machine, El Ajedrecista at the Exposition Universelle in Paris. It used electromagnets and was fully automated. El Ajedrecista automatically played a simple chess endgame of king and rook versus king. The machine required no human intervention once it was set up—it autonomously made legal chess moves and if the human opponent made an illegal move, the machine would signal the error. If the machine was placed in a winning position, it was able to checkmate the human opponent reliably.

In 1951, Marvin Minsky and Dean Edmund build the first artificial neural network. The Stochastic Neural Analog Reinforcement Calculator (SNARC) is an early attempt to model learning processes in the human brain, specifically through reinforcement learning.

SNARC is designed to simulate the behavior of a rat navigating a maze. The idea is to have the machine mimic the way animals learn through rewards and punishment—adjusting its behavior over time based on feedback. It is an analog computer using a network of 3000 vacuum tubes alongside synaptic weights to simulate 40 neuron-like units.

In 1980, WABOT-2, a humanoid robot developed at Waseda University in Japan, is built starting in 1980 and completed around 1984. It followed WABOT-1, which had been built in 1973. While WABOT-1 focused on basic mobility and communication, WABOT-2 is more specialized, designed specifically as a musician robot. It can read musical scores with its camera “eyes,” converse with humans, play music on an electronic organ and even accompany a human singer. This project represents a meaningful step toward the development of humanoid robots and AI capable of performing complex, human-like tasks such as artistic expression.

In 2020, OpenAI introduces GPT-3, a language model with 175 billion parameters, making it one of the largest and most sophisticated AI models to date. GPT-3 demonstrates the ability to generate human-like text, engage in conversations, write code, translate languages and generate creative writing based on natural language prompts. As one of the earliest examples of an LLM, GPTs massive size and scale enabled it to perform a wide variety of language tasks with little to no task-specific training. This example demonstrated the potential of AI to understand and produce highly coherent language.

DeepMind’s AlphaFold 2 makes a breakthrough in biology by accurately predicting the 3D structures of proteins from their amino acid sequences. This achievement solves a problem that stumped scientists for decades—understanding how proteins fold into their unique three-dimensional shapes. AlphaFold 2’s high accuracy in protein structure prediction has implications for disease research and drug development, offering new ways to understand the molecular mechanisms behind illnesses and to design novel therapeutics more efficiently.

In 2021, Google introduces MUM (Multitask Unified Model), a powerful AI system trained across 75 languages and designed to understand information across different types of content, including text and images. MUM is designed to help Google Search handle more complex questions and information needs. The model represents a step toward AI systems that can work across different types of information rather than treating each type of content separately.

Tesla expands access to its Full Self-Driving (FSD) Beta, an advanced driver-assistance system that uses neural networks to handle tasks such as steering, braking and navigation. The system still requires an attentive driver and does not enable a fully autonomous vehicle. Its development reflects the growing use of AI in real-world systems that operate outside traditional software environments.

OpenAI introduces DALL·E, a model that can generate images from text descriptions. DALL·E 2 follows in 2022 with improved image quality and editing capabilities, helping establish text-to-image generation as an important area of generative AI.

In 2023, March: OpenAI releases GPT-4, which demonstrates stronger performance on complex tasks and can accept both text and image inputs. Its release extends the capabilities of the technology behind ChatGPT and accelerates interest in more capable general-purpose AI systems.

Generative AI expands beyond text. Image-generation systems such as DALL·E 3 and other models improve the ability to create detailed images from natural-language prompts.

Google introduces Bard, its conversational AI service, as competition among major technology companies intensifies around generative AI. The launch reflects the growing availability of AI assistants as consumer-facing products.

In 2024, February: Google renames Bard as Gemini and introduces Gemini Advanced, positioning Gemini as the name for its family of generative AI models and products. Google also introduces Gemini 1.5, which brings a substantially longer context window and improved ability to process large amounts of information.

OpenAI introduces Sora, a text-to-video model that can generate realistic video from text prompts. Sora demonstrates the expanding capabilities of generative AI beyond text and still images.

In 2025 AI agents become a major area of development as AI companies introduce systems that can plan tasks, use software and complete multi step workflows. Rather than simply responding to prompts, these systems can take actions on a user’s behalf, making agent AI an important direction for consumer applications and business software.

The open and open-weight AI ecosystem continues to expand. Models such as DeepSeek-R1 demonstrate that highly capable reasoning models can emerge outside the largest proprietary AI platforms. This broadens the model ecosystem and gives developers more options for adapting and deploying AI.

AI adoption also accelerates across enterprise software. Copilots and AI-powered tools become more common in software development, customer service and document processing. Companies integrate AI into broader business workflows rather than treating it as an isolated feature.

AI systems continue to improve at working across text, images, audio and video. Improvements in AI-generated media also expand the range of tasks these systems can support.

In 2026, AI systems continue to improve at reasoning through complex problems and completing multistep tasks. Models are becoming more capable in practical applications such as coding, research and other knowledge work.

AI companies are also working to make advanced models faster and less expensive to run. Smaller and more efficient models can operate on devices such as smartphones and laptops, expanding AI beyond large cloud-based systems and supporting applications where speed, cost or privacy are important.

At the same time, growing AI adoption is driving investment in data centers, specialized chips and other infrastructure. Cost, availability and energy demand are becoming important factors in AI development and deployment.

As AI systems become more autonomous, concerns about reliability, security and control are also growing. In 2026, reports of AI agents taking unintended actions during testing and other deployments have raised questions about how well these systems follow instructions and how much authority they should have over external tools and systems. These concerns are adding urgency to calls for stronger AI safety measures, monitoring and governance as governments and organizations consider how to regulate increasingly capable AI.

Benefits of AI

AI's benefits extend across decision-making, automation, continuous learning, and seamless integration into existing workflows. At its core, AI represents a fundamental shift in how we approach problem-solving and decision-making. In an era defined by unprecedented amounts of data, artificial intelligence serves as our cognitive extension, helping us make sense of complexity that would otherwise overwhelm human capabilities.

Without AI's ability to process and analyze this information, we would be drowning in data while starved for understanding. AI transforms this potential liability into an invaluable asset, extracting meaningful patterns and actionable insights from digital noise.

AI Assistance in education;- Artificial Intelligence systems and tools are already part of our everyday lives. As AI continues to evolve, it is important to be aware of the benefits and the risks it can have in teaching and learning.

Teaching Assistance

AI teaching assistants are specialized systems built specifically for education. These systems can learn your specific course content (textbooks, lecture notes, key concepts) allowing them to give students highly relevant help. A biology AI assistant, for instance, would know the course material inside and out to offer targeted support.

One major advantage of AI teaching assistants is automation. These assistants can grade objective tests, analyze data, and help organize course materials. This automation enables human teachers to focus on the aspects of teaching that require a human touch.

AI teaching assistants also excel at providing answers to objective questions, boosting academic performance through quick, accurate responses. They can explain difficult concepts in multiple ways, walk students through problems step-by-step, and give 

Learning Assistance
Personalized Learning: AI can tailor educational content and pace to individual students’ needs and abilities. It provides customised learning experiences, helping students grasp concepts at their own pace and style, thereby improving comprehension and retention. 

Immediate Feedback: AI-powered systems can provide instant feedback on assignments, quizzes, and tests. 

Accessibility and Inclusivity: AI tools can assist students with disabilities by providing accessible materials, such as text-to-speech or speech-to-text capabilities. These technologies help create a more inclusive learning environment, ensuring that every student has equal access to education. 

Challenges and Concerns 

Data Privacy and Security: AI systems collect and analyse a significant amount of personal data from students and educators. Ensuring the privacy and security of this data, as well as protecting against potential breaches, is a significant concern. 

Ethical Use of AI: Educators and developers need to consider the ethical implications of AI in education, such as bias in algorithms, misinformation, copyright issues, and plagiarism. Striking a balance between AI-driven innovation and responsible use is essential.

Access to Technology: Not all students have equal access to technology and AI-driven resources. Students can be disadvantaged if they don’t have access to AI tools or the necessary technology.

Teacher and Student Over Reliance: There is a risk that teachers and students may become overly dependent on AI for teaching and learning, potentially diminishing critical thinking skills and personal interaction in the classroom.

Bias: As generative AI uses algorithms created by human designers, there is a strong potential for the introduction of bias into the system. Bias occurs where results or outputs from an AI system are disproportionately in favour of or against an idea, group or person. 
Some examples include providing only a limited perspective, showing racial and gender biases. As generative AI uses large data sets, historical and systemic biases are introduced into the system. AI bias is similar to human biases and can lead to inaccurate decisions. The issue of AI bias arises when an algorithm draws conclusions that are too narrow or based on training datasets that do not accurately reflect the population under study.

Inaccuracy: As generative AI draws from large data sets, it is not guaranteed to be accurate as not all information on the internet is accurate. AI is only as good as the data that is fed into the machine learning algorithms. Additionally, generative AI is not necessarily coded to provide accurate information, rather it simply predicts the type of information needed to best fit a given Prompt.

Plagiarism: A common concern from educators is that generative AI and other AI technologies will be used by students to write essays and complete assignments.

Copyright/ Licensing Unknowns: Understanding copyright laws is an important element of using generative AI and other AI technology in the classroom, in that as this is new technology, there are not clear boundaries regarding who owns the content generated by AI. As companies continue to develop licenses on their products, it is important for educators to reflect on the implications of copyright/licensing unknowns.

Development of AI through Business - What does AI do for business?

Digital transformation with AI can yield a variety of benefits for businesses, from cost savings to service delivery. Improving productivity and efficiency top the list of benefits achieved from enterprise AI adoption so far, with two-thirds (66%) of organizations reporting gains. Other benefits organizations reported achieving include, 

Enhancing insights and decision-making (53%)
Reducing costs (40%)
Enhancing client/customer relationships (38%)
Improving products/services and fostering innovation (20%)
Increasing revenue (20%)
Revenue growth largely remains an aspiration, with 74% of organizations hoping to grow revenue through their AI initiatives in the future compared to just 20% that are already doing so. These numbers suggest AI is on the verge of breaking out and delivering a wide range of benefits that go far beyond efficiency and productivity improvements. Ultimately, however, success with AI isn’t just about boosting efficiency or even growing revenue. It’s about achieving strategic differentiation and a lasting competitive edge in the marketplace.

- Will AI replace business development professionals?
No. AI handles data, patterns and content at scale; BD professionals build trust, exercise judgement and navigate stakeholders. The best professionals use AI to amplify those strengths.
- How is AI used in business development?
Mainly for predictive lead scoring, personalized outreach, market intelligence, proposal drafting, pipeline forecasting, relationship intelligence, scenario modelling and partner due diligence.
- What AI skills do BD professionals need?
The BDA BoCK® 2026 embeds four: AI literacy, tool integration, ethics and responsible use, and AI-augmented strategy.
- What are the risks of AI in business development?
Unverified outputs, data privacy breaches, bias in prospect scoring, loss of authenticity with clients, and tools adopted without the competency to use them. Governance and human review reduce these risks.
- How do organizations start using AI in BD?
Follow BDA's four stages: assess, priorities 2–3 use cases, pilot with clear metrics, then scale what works.

What’s the Future of AI for Businesses?

AI in business is constantly evolving. Scalable solutions will require imagination and innovation, with business leaders looking at how entire workflows can be reformulated using AI. Developers with AI experience will be in demand, especially those who understand the ways AI can drive business success. Predicted trends for AI in business include:

- Improved Decision-Making

AI-enabled decision-making is faster and smarter because it can deliver the data business leaders need in real-time. If a marketer wants to know whether to continue a campaign, for example, AI can quickly show how the campaign has performed so far. However, AI may also indicate current market fluctuations and other factors that could impact campaign success. Marketing leaders can access these insights fast and make snap decisions with confidence — based on accurate data. 

- Increased Efficiency

By automating repetitive tasks, AI powers higher efficiency across a range of business processes. Data entry, for example, is time-consuming and not the best use of a skilled professional’s time. When done manually, it’s also prone to human error. AI-powered tools can receive notifications once data changes in one system and automatically update it in related systems. Some AI-enabled systems can even scan handwritten documents and save the writing as text that can later be edited. This is a feature of some modern electronic health record (EHR) systems for healthcare firms. AI ensures that both digital and handwritten records are combined in one place for the benefit of the patient, healthcare providers, and insurers. 

- Enhanced Customer Experience

Customers expect businesses to be available 24/7, regardless of the nature of their queries. However, it’s not always possible to jump on every inquiry instantly. That’s where AI can help. Thanks to natural language processing (NLP) and natural language generation (NLG), chatbots and automated emails can handle many customer queries. Brands can take customer service to the next level by replying to all social media comments in real time. AI can also promptly answer messages or emails and create a highly personalized experience by leveraging NLG.

- Innovation

Generative AI is excellent for brainstorming and creating new ideas that help innovators spark off each other. However, analytical AI can also be a creative force. An understanding of current and future trends that are powered by millions of gigabytes of data helps research and development (R&D) departments. With the right data, they can ensure that they create a perfect product-market fit for a truly competitive advantage. 

- Predictive Analytics

Predictive analytics is a branch of advanced analytics that uses historical data, statistical algorithms, and machine learning techniques to identify the likelihood of future outcomes based on historical data. AI enhances predictive analytics by enabling the analysis of large datasets quickly and accurately, identifying complex patterns that human analysts might miss. This allows businesses to forecast trends, anticipate market changes, and make data-driven decisions with greater confidence. Example: Consider a retail company using predictive analytics to manage inventory. By analyzing historical sales data, customer preferences, and external factors such as seasonal trends, AI can predict which products will be in high demand in the upcoming months. This enables the company to optimize stock levels, reducing both overstock and stockouts. As a result, the company can increase sales, improve customer satisfaction by always having the right products available, and reduce costs associated with excess inventory.
